3D Scene Studio

1Executive summary

3D Scene Studio is a new scene3d layer type that turns Vectura into a plotter-native 3D still-life tool — and, over time, the successor to spiralizer, polyhedron, and topoform.

Today, three separate algorithms each own one shape, one look, and one bag of controls. A torus can spiral or contour or facet — never toggle between them — and no algorithm knows about any other object, any light, or any shadow. Meanwhile the inspiration board (voxel cities with kilometer-long hatched shadows, wood-grain cubes whose tops share one continuous grain, dash-shimmer still lifes in four pens) is all about scenes: multiple objects, real lighting, and per-surface styling decisions.

3D Scene Studio makes four moves:

1 · Objects become a scene. Parametric primitives (box, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus, capsule, the full polyhedron solid family, plane) placed, scaled, and rotated in one scene with a ground plane, a backdrop, and a camera. Primitives stay parametric forever — a torus is two radius sliders, never an editable mesh. STL import (auto-segmented into selectable patches) follows in Phase 4.

2 · The three algorithms become mappers, and styles become stacks. A surface style is just a polyline generator on a surface: spiral (spiralizer), facet (polyhedron), contour (topoform), wireframe, hatch, rings, and the whole 2D pattern/texture library (topo, wavetable, dots, truchet, flowfield, halftone…) draped over faces. A Plot Style is an ordered stack of Ink Passes — each reading Target → Driver → Action → Pen — so one torus can wear base contours, a thick silhouette, purple shadow hatch, ring highlights, and a four-pen cycle at once. One selected torus still toggles spiral ↔ facet ↔ contour ↔ wireframe ↔ textured with one click — and every family remembers its tuning (the style stash).

3 · Light manufactures styleable targets. Lights don't shade lines; they create regions — tone bands, cast shadows, specular hotspots — that you can click and style like anything else. "Shadows on the ground = purple lines at 45° with 2 mm gaps" is four clicks. Highlights can swap patterns, or modify the existing lines: wider spacing, priority thinning, or radial perforation at a chosen density.

4 · Strokes divide into pens. A universal stroke-division treatment cuts any line work into repeating multi-pen dash cycles (A–B–A–C, weighted random, phase jitter, butt-joined or gapped) — physical geometry per pen, not a dash pattern — with pen lifts, travel, and plot time reported per pen before export. It mounts on every layer type, not just scenes.

Why we win: the heavy machinery already exists in the codebase — the shared 3D kernel (geometry3d.js: projection, silhouettes, three hidden-line engines, Lambert hatching), the 25-type region fill engine, two boolean stacks, per-path pen overrides, and the shared fill control surface that two panels already mount. The plan below reuses them by extract-and-rewire (with visual-baseline gates), and is honest about the genuinely new work: scene assembly, shadow projection, the region compiler, the style cascade, stroke division, and a scene-scope depth-buffer API.

Selection is the product. V (the existing select tool) picks whole objects; A (the existing direct-select tool) picks faces, edges, and regions — zero new keybindings, exactly the Illustrator convention the app already follows. If you can select it, you can style it: objects, faces, named face groups, edge classes, individual edges, tone bands, and every cast shadow.

The test of success: a plotter artist who has never touched a 3D app drops three boxes, drags the shadow until the light is right, clicks the shadow, makes it purple hatch at 2 mm, and plots — without opening a single dialog.

3The model: five nouns

A look is never baked into geometry. Geometry produces targets; lighting produces more targets; styles resolve onto targets through a cascade; mappers turn (target + style) into polylines; modify passes reshape them; treatments turn polylines into pen-ready strokes.

1Sceneobjects · lights · ground · backdrop · camera · face groups
2Regionstone bands · cast shadows · specular hotspots — styleable targets made by light
3Cascadeface > group > object > scene; whole-stack, fork-on-override
4Mappersspiral · facet · contour · wireframe · hatch · rings · pattern:* · field:* — plus modify-existing actions (§3.4b)
5Treatmentsstroke divisions — then HLR-safe optimization & per-pen export

3.1 Scene

A scene3d layer owns one scene, fully serialized in layer.params (JSON-safe; regenerates from params; format-versioned with a migration slot). Nodes:

  • Objects — parametric primitives: box, sphere (incl. superellipsoid exponents), cylinder, cone, torus, torus knot, capsule, plane, and the full polyhedron solid family (platonic, geodesic, Goldberg dual, prism/antiprism, cupola, star) with its deformers (expand, twist, explode, extrude, shard) as object-level params. Transform = position, yaw/pitch/roll, scale. Internally each object is a SceneMesh: a welded mesh (drives HLR, hit-testing, shadows) plus the analytic (u,v) chart where one exists (drives exact curved-surface fills).
  • Lights — v1: one or more directional lights (all eight inspiration pieces are directional; point/spot are v2). A light = azimuth/elevation + tone thresholds + castShadows. Each light carries a type field ('directional' in v1) so point/spot lights extend the schema without migration.
  • Ground & Backdrop — first-class styleable receivers, default scene members. The backdrop is the infinite "sky" behind everything. Paper color is a document-level preview setting (dark-stock work).
  • Camera — isometric presets + ortho/perspective toggle; reuses resolveProjection/projectPoint and the existing on-canvas orbit gizmo.
  • Face groups — named face sets. continuous:true requires parallel faces (shared normal — coplanarity is sufficient, not required, so stacked block tops group fine); the group compiles to one shared planar chart: fill generated once over the union, clipped per member face, fragments re-stamped with their faceId.
  • Assets & instances — imported meshes live in a content-hashed asset table referenced by id; objects may instance shared geometry (ten duplicated blocks reference one mesh); history snapshots clone refs, not blobs. Cap ≈8k faces with a warning.
  • Determinism — all sampling, region extraction, seeds, and pen sequences are deterministic: the same scene + seed + version produces the same paths and the same SVG.

Stable identity. Primitives emit deterministic semantic face IDs (box→f:top…; torus→f:outer/f:inner + (u,v) addressing). Imported meshes are segmented at import into dihedral-bounded patches — patches are the selectable unit, raw triangles never are. Edge IDs are canonical sorted vertex-pair keys. On topology change, orphaned styles invalidate loudly ("3 styles lost their faces — reassign?"), never silently remap. Every emitted path carries meta:{objectId, faceId, role, regionClass?} — the same meta channel hit-testing, transforms, and export already consume.

3.2 Regions & drivers — lighting as a target factory

Both proposals converged on the same move independently, which is strong evidence it is right: lighting produces named, styleable inputs — never pixels. The merged model unifies this doc's tone bands and density drivers with the Ink-Pass proposal's driver taxonomy: a pass's Driver is one of always · tone band · castShadow · specular · distance-to-edge · radial · noise (depth, facing, and curvature are v2). The v1 mechanisms behind each driver are the judged, feasibility-verified ones below.

  • Tone bands. Lambert I = N·L quantized at user thresholds into named buckets (highlight / lit / shade / core). Flat faces: constant I → whole-face band assignment (trivial, exact). Curved faces: Winkenbach priority modulation — fills generate as prioritized stroke families; bands thin/cull priorities in highlights and add the cross-pass in shadows. No fragile iso-band polygons on curved meshes in v1; the specular hotspot is the one exemption (small, closed → clipping rings to it is cheap, satisfying "highlights = concentric rings" on a sphere).
  • Tone is calibrated in ink, not millimetres. Band response is expressed as coverage % computed from the pen's width; a one-click tone ladder test swatch calibrates any pen. Preview renders true pen widths on the paper color.
  • Cast shadows. Occluder silhouettes projected along the light direction onto ground/receiver faces. Shadows union within style-equivalence classes: a caster-bound style ("make this shadow purple") subtracts its caster's projection from the general union and fills separately; styled wins where they overlap. All booleans route through FillBoolean.safeOp (degrade = drop region; never crash generate()).
  • Density drivers (v1 minimal). A modify pass's driver can read one scalar field — distanceToRegionEdge, radialFromPoint, or noise — driving spacing, cull rate, or perforation (region-masked gap cutting: "gaps in a radial pattern of a given density", verbatim from the brief). Planar faces + receiver regions in v1; full driver plumbing v2.
  • Stable addressing. Region styles bind to (receiver, regionClass[, casterId]) tuples — never polygon instances — so styles survive the light moving, shadows merging and splitting.

3.3 The cascade — Plot Styles as Ink Pass stacks

A Plot Style is an ordered stack of Ink Passes, not a single render-mode enum (adopted from the parallel Ink-Pass proposal — it raises the creative ceiling decisively). Every pass reads as one sentence: Target → Driver → Action → Pen. A collapsed pass card reads "Shadow · Hatch 45° · 2 mm · Purple". One torus can stack: base contours + thick silhouette + purple shadow hatch + ring highlights + dashed hidden lines + a four-pen cycle — each pass independently editable, reorderable, and hideable. Overlapping add-passes simply overprint — both draw, deterministically, in stack order; Knockout/Interleave combination modes are v2.

styleTable: {                                  // scope → Plot Style (whole stack)
  scene: PlotStyle,
  byObject:  { objectId: PlotStyle },
  byGroup:   { faceGroupId | smartSetId: PlotStyle },
  byFace:    { "objectId/faceId": PlotStyle },
  byRegion:  { "receiver/regionClass[/casterId]": PlotStyle },   // "ground/castShadow"
  byEdge:    { edgeClass | "objectId/edgeClass" | "objectId/edgeId": EdgeStyle }
}
PlotStyle = { passes: [InkPass, …] }           // ordered; new-object template: Base · Tone · Edges · Pen Cycle
InkPass   = { target,                          // surface | region class | edge class | smart set
              driver,                          // always | tone band | castShadow | specular | distance | radial | noise (v2: depth, facing, curvature)
              action,                          // ADD strokes (a mapper: family, space, params)
                                               //   or MODIFY existing (spacing× · cull · perforate · weight · pen-swap · jitter)
              penId }
EdgeStyle = { penId, weightMm,                 // rendered as N parallel offset passes — physically honest thickness
              dash, hiddenTreatment }          // hidden: drop | dash | ghost

Legibility guardrails (the judged rules survive the stack model). The cascade resolves the whole stack, most-specific-wins: face > group/set > object > scene. Overriding forks the full stack — an explicit "Detach" moment; the inspector always shows one provenance chip ("Styled by: Object · Wood Grain" with ✕-to-clear). No per-pass inheritance across scopes in v1: "why is this face purple?" must always have one answer. New objects start from a fixed four-card template (Base Surface · Tone response · Edges · Pen Cycle), so the default experience stays exactly as simple as the old single-material model — the stack is there when you reach for it. One clarification keeps the card model honest: the Pen Cycle card is a front-end for the style's division treatment (§3.5) — whatever its position in the stack, it compiles to the division stage and always executes last (G-04).

Swatches are copy-on-apply presets (Wood Grain, Blueprint Hatch, Dash Shimmer, Solid Purple Shadow ship as starters). Editing a swatch never retroactively edits objects. Style DNA copy/paste and an Alt-click eyedropper move whole stacks between targets. Each object also keeps a style stash: every mapper family's params persist on the base pass, so toggling spiral→contour→spiral restores your twelve turns of tuning. Looks (named A/B snapshots of a whole stack for one object) are a P2 fast-follow.

3.4 Actions I: add-stroke mappers

An Ink Pass's Action is either add strokes (a mapper) or modify existing strokes (§3.4b). A mapper is a pure polyline generator on a surface, fully decoupled from visibility (fogleman/ln's architecture). Mapper params extend the fill-record schema — the 2D subset mounts on the existing FillControlSurface; native params (turns, slice-plane, band count) register alongside.

Space discipline. Screen-space fields are one global 2D field clipped against the merged visible-silhouette union per (object × tone band) — never per facet — so a plainly hatched sphere stays a family of long strokes (pen lifts matter). Chart-space fills are mm-honest on planar faces; on analytic charts distortion is bounded and declared. Fill-record granularity: one record per style-equivalence class of faces (regions = the class's union); fragments are re-stamped with faceId during the per-face clip pass; tone override splits the class (accepted, documented discontinuity), tone modulate is a post-op on the shared field's output so global fields never break.

3.4b Actions II: modify-existing strokes

The second action family operates on strokes another pass already produced — this is the brief's "apply an effect to the existing lines" verbatim, and the Ink-Pass proposal's taxonomy names it well. v1 ships: spacing × (Winkenbach priority thin/densify), cull %, perforate (region-masked gap cutting at a driver-set density — "gaps in a radial pattern of a given density"), weight ×, and pen swap. v2 adds jitter, warp, shorten, and suppress-below-length. Modify passes run on stroke samples before visibility, so they compose with every mapper.

3.5 Treatments

divideStroke — a new core primitive: walk true arc length, cut into a repeating cycle of classes [{lenMm, penId}, {gapMm}, …], each sub-path stamped meta.penId. Grammar: ordered cycles (A–B–A–C), weighted-random pen choice, phase fixed / per-path / jitter, butt-join default (pen lifts only on true gaps), gaps as a first-class "pen named none". Phase is computed in pre-clip arc length — the unbroken source path (or the group chart) stamps every fragment's offset, so color sequences flow seamlessly across face seams and occlusion gaps. Divisions mount on every layer type as an optional treatment (flowfield + divisions is inspiration piece 6, no scene needed).

Division data flow (explicit contract): division runs as a final engine-side display-pipeline stage after engine.optimizeLayers (simplify → sort → parent-path dedupe), chain-aware so pen classes sort as ordered chains; one divided output feeds canvas, preview, plot statistics, and export identically. The SVG export fix — grouping by effective per-path pen, dedupe re-keyed by effective pen — lands first as its own regression-covered PR.

5Interaction design

5.1 Selection: V and A, specialized — zero new bindings

V already activates the select tool and A the direct-select tool (src/ui/shortcuts.js:211/221). When a scene3d layer is active they specialize — exactly the Illustrator convention users already have:

  • Object mode (V): click picks a whole scene object via its projected path bundle (meta.objectId, nearest depth wins). Standard bounds; the existing 3D orbit gizmo targets the selected object — with nothing selected it orbits the camera. Scene layers suppress the 2D layer-transform handles (no dual-meaning drags).
  • Component mode (A): click picks a face (point-in-projected-polygon, nearest first); edges hit within a screen-px tolerance win over faces; Alt-click cycles deeper through faces → regions → occluded targets, with a "Cast shadow (ground) · 2 of 3" readout in the context bar. If a region already carries a style, it is preferred on first hit. Marquee selects intersecting faces; Shift adds; plain marquee = front faces, Alt-marquee includes occluded ones ("all six sides").
  • Select Similar → Smart Sets (right-click): same normal / facing up · same object · same group · same style · coplanar-touching · shadowed-by (v2). Scoped to the selected objects by default — "expand to scene" is the secondary action, so "all tops" never accidentally grabs the floor. Any Select Similar result can be saved as a Smart Set — a live predicate that re-evaluates when the scene changes (add a tenth block: it joins "Tops" automatically) — or frozen as a static set. Smart Sets may span objects and are style targets like any face group.
  • Group Selection: any multi-face selection surfaces a ⛓ button in the context bar — name it (static set or live Smart Set), optionally mark it continuous (with a live parallel-faces validity check). This is the face-group birth story; selecting is not grouping.
  • Double-click an object enters it (existing isolation breadcrumb); Esc walks out. Isolation is for crowded scenes, never a prerequisite — A works directly on the scene.

5.2 Building and posing

  • Primitive shelf (panel, Scene tab): one row of icon chips — click drops at origin, drag places a ghost on the ground with drag-to-size. Feels like the existing shape tools, not a 3D app.
  • Pose: plain drag moves in the ground plane (the 95% case); Shift-drag lifts vertically with a live footprint circle; corner handles scale; rotation via the existing orbit gizmo.
  • Camera: iso presets (NE/NW/SE/SW, Front, Top) + ortho/perspective toggle + free orbit. No fly modes, ever.

5.3 Light: the shadow is the handle

Azimuth/elevation mean nothing to a 2D artist; "the shadow goes there and is this long" means everything. So the primary manipulation is dragging the shadow's tip — direction sets azimuth, length sets elevation — with the sun widget following as feedback (Demo ①). During a drag, shadows render as flat draft tints from ground-projection only (no polygon booleans → nothing can flicker); full regions and fills regenerate debounced on release.

5.4 One style surface

Selecting anything morphs the context bar to a scene context with five mapper-family chips — one click swaps the family, the style stash preserves each family's tuning. The chip's caret opens the Style popover: a FillControlSurface mount (extended with the mapper families' native params + pen row + tone + divisions). The panel's Style tab mounts the same schema on the same params bag — chips and popover are shortcuts into it, never parallel implementations (the paint-bucket panel-doubling lesson). A radial pie menu is a P2 fast-follow; chips already make toggling one click.

6.2 Visibility strategy (the crux, specified honestly)

  • Flat-face fast path (default for polyhedral scenes): z-sorted projected face polygons clip farther paths via segmentPathByPolygons (crash-proof, exact). Occluder depth is computed per sample from the face's support plane at (x,y) — never one scalar z per face (large ground-plane receivers and coplanar contacts break scalar sorting). Ground and backdrop are universal never-occluders. Auto-fallback to the depth buffer when two objects' depth ranges overlap ambiguously.
  • Scene depth buffer (curved/mixed scenes): this is new API work, not a promotion — today's occludeSegmentsDepthBuffer takes one global mode/bias and has no owner concept. Phase 1 builds: rasterize the scene's triangle soup once per frame into a reusable buffer exposing depthAt(x,y); segments carry {ownerId, mode, bias}; owner-aware bias prevents self-occlusion acne.
  • See-through v1 = binary + dash: per-object solid | xray (hidden runs dropped or dashed — today's hiddenLineMode conventions). Quantitative invisibility (per-level styling) is v2, restricted to the flat-face path where counting occluders is cheap.

6.4 Performance contract

  • Dirty-flag DAG from day one (not a late phase): camera dirties projection+; a light dirties regions only; a style edit dirties one class's fills; an object transform dirties that object + shadows it casts. Per-stage caches keyed on signature hashes (compound-cache precedent).
  • Draft mode is structural: flat tints for regions, cull-only visibility, decimated sampling during drags; full quality debounced on release (SETTINGS.preview3dQuality hook).
  • Mesh interning: content-hashed asset table outside history clones (cloneLayerParams generalized); release-only snapshots. NOTE: styleTable still deep-clones per gesture — accepted v1 cost, monitored in the perf suite.
  • No workers in v1 — the app must run from file://. Worker offload of the depth buffer is a v2 enhancement when served over http.
  • Budgets: ≤8k faces/scene (import warning), light-drag ≥20fps in draft, full regen ≤1.5s for a 12-object scene.
  • Complexity is never silently dropped. Before dense operations, the UI shows a path-count/plot-length estimate and offers a preview LOD — the live-app audit found a default wireframe sphere producing ~30,000 paths / 57 m of plotting with no warning; that class of surprise is a release blocker here.

7Requirements

Summary. v1 spans 12 requirement groups (A–L, 107 MUSTs): scene/objects and selection (A–B), the style cascade and mappers (C–D), lighting-made regions (E), visibility (F), stroke divisions (G), panels (H), performance and serialization (I–J), export and testing (K–L). The riskiest clusters are the shadow booleans (E-07…E-09), the depth-buffer API (F-04/F-05), and the Phase 0C extraction (L-02). Point/spot lights, OBJ, quantitative x-ray, and full density plumbing form the v2 line. Requirements are written in Simplified Technical English: short sentences, active voice, one requirement per item. Priorities: MUST ships in v1 · SHOULD ships in v1 if the phase stays on budget · V2 is deferred and flagged. Every MUST maps to a phase in §8 and at least one test class.

A · Scene and objects

A-01mustThe scene3d layer must hold many objects, one or more directional lights, one ground plane, one backdrop, and one camera.
A-02mustThe primitive set must include: box, sphere, superellipsoid, cylinder, cone, torus, torus knot, capsule, plane, and the polyhedron solid family (platonic, geodesic, dual, prism, antiprism, cupola, star).
A-03mustEach primitive must stay parametric. The user must not edit a primitive as a raw mesh.
A-04mustEach object must have position, yaw, pitch, roll, and scale.
A-05mustEach object must build a SceneMesh: a welded mesh, plus an analytic (u,v) chart when one exists.
A-06mustPolyhedron deformers (expand, twist, explode, extrude, shard) must be object-level parameters on the solid family.
A-07mustEvery face must have a deterministic ID that is stable across regeneration.
A-08mustEvery edge must have a deterministic ID from its sorted vertex pair.
A-09mustWhen topology changes, the system must invalidate orphaned styles with a visible message. It must not remap them silently.
A-10amustSTL import must land in Phase 4.
A-10bmustThe importer must segment the mesh into dihedral-bounded patches.
A-10cmustPatches are the selectable unit for imported meshes.
A-10dmustSTL import must present a repair sheet: units, up axis, centering, normal repair / weld tolerance, and preview simplification.
A-11mustImported meshes must live in a content-hashed asset table. History snapshots must clone references, not mesh data.
A-12mustThe system must warn at 8,000 faces per scene.
A-13shouldDrag-and-drop of an STL file onto the canvas should import it into the active scene.
A-14v2OBJ import (named groups become face groups); glTF; SVG-extrude; point and spot lights.
A-15mustEach light must carry a type field. v1 implements directional. The schema must accept new light types without a format migration.
A-16mustObjects must be able to instance a shared mesh asset. Ten duplicates must reference one mesh, in memory and in undo snapshots.
A-17mustGeneration must be deterministic: the same scene, seed, and version must produce the same paths and the same SVG.

B · Selection

B-01amustWith a scene layer active, the select tool (V) must pick whole objects.
B-01bmustWith a scene layer active, the direct tool (A) must pick faces, edges, and regions.
B-01cmustThe feature must not add new global keybindings.
B-02mustFace hit-testing must use point-in-projected-polygon with nearest depth first.
B-03mustEdge hit-testing must use a screen-pixel distance threshold. Edges must win over faces inside the threshold.
B-04mustAlt-click must cycle through overlapping targets (face → region → occluded). The context bar must show the cycle position ("2 of 3").
B-05mustWhen a region already has a style, a click must prefer the region.
B-06mustMarquee in component mode must select intersecting front faces. Alt-marquee must include occluded faces.
B-07mustSelect Similar must offer: same normal, same object, same group, same style, coplanar-touching. The default scope is the selected objects. "Expand to scene" is a secondary action.
B-07bmustA Select Similar result must be savable as a live Smart Set (the predicate re-evaluates on scene change) or a frozen static set. Sets may span objects and are style targets.
B-07cmustThe component submode (edge / face) must be visible in the status readout ("A · Face"). Pressing A again cycles submodes; 2/3 select directly. Vertex submode is v2.
B-08mustA multi-face selection must show a "Group Selection" action in the context bar. The action names the group and offers the continuous toggle with a live validity check.
B-09mustDouble-click must enter an object (isolation breadcrumb). Component selection must also work without isolation.
B-10mustSelection state must restore with undo snapshots.

C · Plot Styles, Ink Passes, and the cascade

C-01mustOne styleTable per scene must hold entries at scopes: scene, object, face group / smart set, face, region tuple, and edge (class, object/class, object/edge).
C-02mustResolution must be whole-stack, most-specific-wins: face > group/set > object > scene.
C-03mustAn override must fork the full Plot Style stack. The inspector must show one provenance chip with a clear-override control.
C-04amustA Plot Style must be an ordered stack of Ink Passes. Passes must support reorder, hide, duplicate, and delete.
C-04bmustEvery Ink Pass must declare: Target, Driver, Action, and Pen. A collapsed pass card must read as that sentence.
C-04cmustActions divide into add-strokes (a mapper) and modify-existing (spacing ×, cull %, perforate, weight ×, pen swap in v1).
C-04dmustNew objects must start from the fixed four-card template: Base Surface, Tone response, Edges, Pen Cycle.
C-04emustMapper params extend the fill-record schema.
C-05mustRegion styles must bind to (receiver, regionClass, optional casterId) tuples. They must not bind to polygon instances.
C-06mustAn EdgeStyle must hold: pen, weight in mm, dash, and hidden treatment (drop, dash, or ghost).
C-07mustEdge weight must render as N parallel offset passes. The silhouette class must default heavier than creases.
C-08mustEach object must keep a style stash: the last params of every mapper family. A family toggle must restore that family's stash.
C-09mustSwatches must be copy-on-apply presets. A swatch edit must not change objects that used it.
C-10mustStyle copy/paste must move the whole stack between targets. Alt-click with an open popover must sample a target's stack (eyedropper).
C-11shouldPer-face and per-assignment random seeds should be editable ("re-seed this face's grain").
C-12v2Looks: named A/B snapshots of a whole stack per object, switchable without duplicating geometry.

D · Mappers

D-01mustThe v1 mapper registry must include: hatch, contour, spiral/meridians, facet, wireframe/trimesh, rings, pattern:*, field:*, and none.
D-02mustEach mapper must declare its space: chart or screen.
D-03mustScreen-space fields must clip against the merged visible-silhouette union per (object × tone band). They must not clip per facet.
D-04mustpattern:* and field:* mappers must work on planar faces and on analytic-chart primitives (torus, sphere, cylinder, cone). Arbitrary meshes are planar-only in v1.
D-05mustThe rings mapper must draw concentric rings about a draggable center on backdrop, ground, and planar regions.
D-06mustThe spiral mapper must keep spiralizer's controls: turns, twist turns, line count, render/marker styles.
D-07mustThe contour mapper must keep topoform's slice-plane orientation controls.
D-08mustOne fill record must serve each style-equivalence class of faces. The per-face clip pass must re-stamp fragments with their faceId.
D-09mustA continuous face group must generate one fill in the shared chart and clip it per member face. Member faces must be parallel (shared normal); coplanarity is not required.
D-10shouldThe system should offer a noise-rack displacement post-op on fill outputs (grain warble, hand wobble).

E · Lighting and regions

E-01mustLambert tone must quantize into user-named bands at user thresholds (default: highlight, lit, shade, core).
E-02mustOn flat faces, the whole face must take its constant-tone band.
E-03amustOn curved faces, bands must modulate prioritized stroke families: thin or cull in highlights, add the cross-pass in shadows.
E-03bmustv1 must not build iso-band polygons on curved meshes. The specular hotspot is the one exception.
E-04mustThe specular hotspot region must accept region-shaped fills (for example, concentric rings) on curved and flat surfaces.
E-05mustEach band must either modulate the base passes' output or override it with its own add-stroke pass.
E-06mustThe system must express tone response as ink coverage %, computed from pen width. The tone-ladder generator must output a labeled swatch grid at fixed coverage steps (10–90%) for any pen.
E-07mustCast shadows must project occluder silhouettes along the light direction onto the ground and receiver faces.
E-08amustShadows must union within each style-equivalence class.
E-08bmustA caster-bound style must subtract its caster's projection from the general union.
E-08cmustThe styled fill wins where a styled and an unstyled shadow overlap.
E-09mustAll area booleans must run through safeOp. A failed boolean must drop the region. It must never crash generation.
E-10mustA modify pass must accept one scalar driver: distance-to-region-edge, radial-from-point, or noise. The driver must drive spacing, cull rate, or perforation. v1 scope: planar faces and receiver regions. (Depth, facing, and curvature drivers are v2.)
E-11mustPerforation must cut region-masked gaps into existing strokes at a given density and gap length.
E-12v2Specular spill onto neighbor faces; point/spot lights with falloff; penumbra ramps; full density-field plumbing. (Spill needs receiver-side reflected-ray region generation — genuinely new machinery no inspiration piece depends on; the hotspot-on-object half of the ask ships in v1 via E-04.)

F · Visibility

F-01mustPolyhedral scenes must use the flat-face fast path: z-sorted projected face polygons clipping farther paths.
F-02mustFast-path occluder depth must come from the face's support plane at each sample point, not one scalar per face.
F-03mustGround and backdrop must never occlude.
F-04mustThe scene depth buffer must rasterize the scene's triangles once per frame and expose a depthAt(x,y) query. Segments must carry {ownerId, mode, bias}.
F-05mustThe system must fall back from the fast path to the depth buffer when object depth ranges overlap ambiguously.
F-06mustEach object must be solid or x-ray. Hidden runs must drop (solid) or dash (x-ray).
F-07v2Quantitative invisibility (style per occlusion level), flat-face path only.

G · Treatments, pens, divisions

G-01mustdivideStroke must cut a polyline into a repeating cycle of {length, pen} and {gap} classes by true arc length in mm.
G-02mustThe grammar must support: ordered cycles, weighted-random pen choice, phase fixed / per-path / jitter, butt-join (default), and true gaps.
G-03amustThe engine must compute phase in pre-clip arc length.
G-03bmustFragments must inherit their source-path offset, so color sequences continue across face seams and occlusion gaps.
G-04mustDivision must run as the final engine stage, after simplify, sort, and parent-path dedupe. One divided output must feed canvas, preview, plot stats, and export.
G-05mustDivided pen classes must sort as ordered chains. They must not be exempt from travel optimization.
G-06mustDivisions must mount as an optional treatment on every layer type, with the same editor.
G-07mustSub-paths must carry meta.penId. The renderer and exporter must honor it.

H · Panels and controls

H-01mustThe scene panel must use the bespoke-panel hook with three tabs: Scene (shelf, tree, inspector), Style (cascade + edge classes + divisions), Output (camera, quality, plot stats).
H-02mustAll style-editing surfaces (context-bar chips, popover, Style tab) must mount one control schema on one params bag.
H-03mustFillControlSurface must gain registered sections for mapper families, pen, tone, and divisions. Paint-bucket and Text hosts must pass regression tests.
H-04mustThe orbit gizmo must target the selected object. With no selection, it must orbit the camera.
H-05mustThe shadow tip must be draggable: direction sets azimuth, length sets elevation. During the drag, shadows must render as flat tints. Full fills regenerate on release.
H-06mustThe scene tree must reuse the layers-panel nested rendering. Tree and canvas selection must stay in sync.
H-07mustPaper color must be a document-level preview setting. Preview must render true pen widths on the paper color.
H-08shouldNew-layer defaults: one sun, ground, backdrop, one starter object, and a hint card.
H-09v2Radial pie menu; on-face hatch-angle vane; "distribute selected pens across cycle" quick action.

I · Performance

I-01mustDirty-flag caching must ship with Phase 1: camera, light, style, and transform edits must each recompute only their downstream stages.
I-02mustLight drags must hold 20 fps or better in draft mode on a 12-object scene.
I-03mustFull regeneration of a 12-object scene must finish within 1.5 s on the perf-suite reference machine (the CI perf runner).
I-04mustv1 must not require web workers. The app must keep working from file://.
I-05mustThe system must never silently drop complexity. Before dense operations, it must show a path-count and plot-length estimate and offer a preview LOD.

J · Serialization and migration

J-01mustThe whole scene (objects, lights, camera, groups, styleTable, assets) must serialize inside layer.params as JSON-safe data.
J-02mustThe scene schema must carry its own version and a migration entry in the format chain.
J-03mustcloneLayerParams must handle the asset table generically (clone by reference into snapshots).
J-04mustA user-presets/scene3d/ directory and ALGO_DEFAULTS.scene3d must exist. Defaults live in config, never in the algorithm.
J-05mustOld spiralizer, polyhedron, and topoform layers must keep loading unchanged.

K · Export and plot physics

K-01mustSVG export must group paths by effective per-path pen, with regression tests over compound, morph, and mask documents.
K-02mustExport dedupe must key by effective pen.
K-03mustThe Output tab must show pen lifts, travel distance, and estimated plot time per pen before export.
K-04shouldPer-pen registration marks should be an export option.
K-05mustMinimum-segment and minimum-gap guards must warn when division output violates the active machine profile, with the violating fragment count.

L · Testing and docs

L-01mustEvery phase must pass its test-matrix gate: unit (geometry, regions, division), integration (panel, selection), e2e (flows), visual (hero scenes), perf (drag loop).
L-02mustPhase 0C refactors must keep the three legacy algorithms' visual baselines unchanged.
L-03mustEach behavior change must have a test that fails without it (repo RGR rule).
L-04mustDocs contract per repo rules: README, CHANGELOG, plans.md, in-app help, version sync.

8.0 Release gates (every phase, non-negotiable)

Adopted from the Ink-Pass proposal's doctrine — its live audit proved that visible controls cannot be assumed to equal dependable capability (the June 3D audit's silent no-ops). Three "truths" gate every release:

  • Visual truth — every surfaced control changes a reviewed output under a representative scene; dense output cannot conceal a nominal effect.
  • Plot truth — pen groups, physical gaps, stats, optimization, sort order, and SVG all agree on the same effective path pen.
  • Compatibility truth — historical layers and presets stay loadable; conversion is explicit; schema migrations are versioned and tested.

8.1 Dependencies

0A → (G-gates in 4A/4B) · 0B → 4A · 0C → 1A → {2A, 3A} · 0D → 1B → 3A · 1C → 3C · 2A → 4B driver · all → 4C presets. Streams inside a phase are file-disjoint by construction; collisions found in scouting re-partition before implementation (repo rule).

8.2 Hero demos (ship with presets; each is a marketing asset and a test)

  1. Golden Hour — voxel city, six exported frames as the light sweeps; shadows merge and stretch.
  2. Same Torus, Five Ways — the succession gate: spiral / facet / contour / wire-xray / textured, one object.
  3. Disco Still Life — the piece-4 homage: 4-pen dashed rings, solid purple shadows, dashed-ring backdrop, dark stock.
  4. Woodshop — stacked blocks, one continuous knotted grain across all tops, independent sides.
  5. X-Ray Machine — one glass object among solids, interior dashed, specular hotspot ringed.
  6. One Pen, All Tones — the blue cutaway: a single pen, everything said with density drivers and tone bands.
